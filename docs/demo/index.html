News provenance tracer · English-language news, January to April 2020

Who reported it first?

A story says someone said something, or that something happened. Which outlet had it first? This tracer takes one sentence from a news story and the time it was published, searches everything published before then, and names the earliest report that carried the same quote or the same event.

It works on two archives. For quotes it uses Quotebank, which records every quotation found in millions of 2020 news articles with its outlet and time. For events it uses headlines collected by GDELT, which logs news articles as it finds them. A self-improvement loop then tuned the tracer's settings against a benchmark whose answers are known. Every example below comes from held-out items the loop never saw.

  1. SearchFull-text search over quotes or headlines published before the story, ranked by word overlap.
  2. CheckA small, fast model (Claude Haiku) reads the top candidates and keeps the ones that say the same thing as the sentence.
  3. DateEvery article that carried a kept quote or headline goes on a timeline. The earliest one is the answer.

    Self-improvement loop

    How the tracer tuned itself

    Each round, a large model (Claude Opus) reads the tracer's current settings and the cases it got wrong on the training items, then proposes up to three small changes: a different search, more candidates, rewritten instructions for the checker. Cheap screens weed out the weak ones. The best one or two are scored on all training items, and one is kept only if it fixes clearly more items than it breaks (more than run-to-run noise) and does not do worse on the validation items. The watch set and the test set stay out of the loop.

    Method and caveats

    How the answers are known, and what they miss

    Quotes: ground truth from the archive itself

    • Benchmark items are quotes carried by at least five outlets. The sentence the tracer gets is a reworded version of a later outlet's report; the answer is the earliest timed mention of that quote before it.
    • Near-identical versions of the same quote by the same speaker count as the same quote, so an earlier rendering is a correct answer, not a false one.
    • Quotebank keeps the quote, a short passage around it, the outlet, the URL and the time. It has no full article text, so the tracer can follow quotes but not facts reported without one.
    • AP and Reuters' own sites are missing from Quotebank, so the “first” outlet is often the fastest site that ran wire copy.
    • Mentions with only a date and no time of day are shown but never ranked as first.

    Events: labelled by a model, then audited

    • There is no official record of who reported an event first, so the answers are model-made. Claude Sonnet 5.5 (low effort) labelled each event's first report from the headlines GDELT saw in the days before the story. An answer counts as right if it is in the first wave: the labelled first report or a report of the same event within 30 minutes after it.
    • A label can only be as early as the headlines the labeller was shown. So every answer that came out earlier than the label went back to the same labeller: the answers of every config the loop scored in full (on train and validation), and of the baseline and the final settings on every split, including test and watch, so those two are scored on the same footing. This is ground truth v3.3.
    • The ground truth is therefore only as good as the labeller. An independent audit by Claude Fable agreed with 9 in 10 of the reviews it re-judged (below), so every event score here carries some label error.
    • GDELT times are when GDELT's crawler first saw an article, in 15-minute steps (UTC), not when it was published. They are an upper bound, and reports found in the same 15-minute step cannot be told apart.
